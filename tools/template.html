1

The physics in the picture

Viscosity lets the moving lid drag the fluid just beneath it, and that sets up a single clockwise primary vortex filling the cavity. That is why the speed map is bright only just below the lid (speed ≈ 1) and dark in the middle. The vortex centre is the point where the velocity is zero.

Re = UL/ν is the ratio of inertia to viscosity; here U = L = 1, so ν = 1/Re. At low Re viscosity dominates and the vortex is nearly symmetric left to right (centre x ≈ 0.55). As Re grows, inertia carries the fluid downstream toward the right wall, and the centre moves right and down (x ≈ 0.61, y ≈ 0.67 at Re 300).

Pressure is high in the top-right corner, where fluid carried by the lid runs into the wall (a stagnation point), and low in the top-left corner, where fluid is pulled away from the wall. The dimensionless pressure p/(ρU²) shrinks as Re grows: viscous pressure scales as μU/L, so after dividing by ρU² it goes as 1/Re. In the CFD, the top-right corner pressure is at Re 20 and at Re 300.

What the network gets right: where the vortex sits and how strong it is

Over the 10 Test cases (Re values held out of training) the error is about 1.5% of the lid speed: RMSE . More importantly, the network follows how the vortex centre moves with Re. One grid cell is 0.02 wide, and the NN centre stays within one cell of the CFD centre in most cases. Vortex strength (the minimum of the streamfunction ψ) matches the CFD to within 3%.

Where the error remains: the lid and the two top corners

Share of the Test-case squared error that falls in the top 10% band (y > 0.9). The band is only a tenth of the area, yet it holds most of the error.

The cause is the corner singularity. Where the lid (u = 1) meets a side wall (u = 0), the velocity jumps, and in theory the pressure becomes unbounded. A smooth function with two tanh hidden layers of 32 nodes cannot represent such sharp peaks, so it flattens them.

The error is largest at the ends of the range, especially the pressure near Re 20, even though that is a Train case. Low-Re pressure peaks are both larger (1/Re scaling) and sharper, while the output normalization uses one standard deviation for all Re, so these extremes carry relatively little weight in training. Cases at the ends of the range also have training neighbours on one side only.

4

Physical laws are not guaranteed

The network was trained only to fit the CFD data with MSE. The continuity equation ∂u/∂x + ∂v/∂y = 0 was never imposed as a constraint; this is not a PINN. Applying the same central difference to the cell-centred values of both fields gives the RMS divergence below (closer to zero means better mass conservation).

The NN field diverges 3–5 times more than the CFD field. The CFD value is not zero only because it is computed from cell-centred averages; on the solver's own MAC grid the divergence is about 10−15. For the same reason, the small secondary eddies in the bottom corners cannot be trusted. In the CFD their strength grows with Re from 4×10−6 to 1×10−4, while the NN gives about 2×10−3 regardless of Re. The streamlines that curl in those corners are error, not physics.

5

Why it can predict a new Re without running CFD

Notebook Check question: once training finishes, why can the model predict a new Reynolds condition without running CFD again?

Re is an input, and the 40 training cases were learned together, so the network has learned one function (x, y, Re) → (u, v, p) that is continuous in Re. A new Re is just a new input to that function. Evaluating it is a fixed-weight matrix product over 2,500 cells, about 1,200 multiplications per cell. One NN field takes about 7 ms; one CFD case needs thousands of time steps and took about 3 s.

This holds only for interpolation inside the training range. Section 6 tests what happens outside it.

6

Outside the training range: Re 300–1000

The network was trained on Re 20–300 and is used here unchanged. For checking only, 15 new CFD cases were solved at Re = 300, 350, …, 1000 with the notebook's own solver. Every case met the notebook's acceptance limits (steady residual < 2×10−5, divergence RMS < 10−8), and the new Re 300 field is identical to the training-set Re 300 field (max difference ), so both data sets come from the same solver. The clearest failure is the vortex centre, which moves the wrong way. In the CFD, beyond Re 300 the centre turns back toward the middle and sinks (x 0.611 → 0.565, y 0.665 → 0.608 from Re 300 to 1000), the same direction as the high-resolution benchmark. The network keeps the trend it saw near Re 300: its centre runs to the right until it reaches the wall (x ≈ 0.99 at Re 950–1000, which no cavity flow does).

Vortex strength is harder to judge. The network's |ψmin| grows about 40% (0.099 → 0.138), this CFD's falls (0.095 → 0.079), and the benchmark rises more gently (0.103 at Re 100 to 0.118 at Re 1000). The falling CFD value is an artefact of its coarse grid (see the note below), so the safe reading is that the network overshoots both references. Close to the boundary the agreement is still reasonable: at Re 350 the velocity RMSE is about 2–3% of the lid speed.

Why it breaks. The network sees Re through the normalization z = (Re − 160) / 82.6, so training covered z = −1.7 to 1.7, and Re 1000 is z = 10.2. That far out, 41% of the first-layer tanh units are saturated (6% at Re 300), and the output is driven by whichever units are still on their slopes. Nothing in the training data tells the network that the vortex must turn back, so it extends the slope it learned. Between Re 900 and 1000 its field still changes about seven times more than the CFD field does (RMS change 0.024 vs 0.003).

About the reference: a 50 × 50 first-order upwind solver is numerically diffusive, so at high Re it behaves like a lower Re. Against the benchmark of Ghia et al., its centre is (0.597, 0.643) vs (0.555, 0.606) at Re 400 and (0.565, 0.608) vs (0.531, 0.563) at Re 1000. It is the right reference for this check because it is the same solver that produced the training data, but it is not a grid-independent solution.

Model: MLP, Tanh, 2 × 32 hidden nodes. CFD: 50 × 50 MAC-grid reference, not grid-independent.
The vortex centre is the minimum of the streamfunction ψ(x, y) = ∫0y u dy, refined by parabolic interpolation.
